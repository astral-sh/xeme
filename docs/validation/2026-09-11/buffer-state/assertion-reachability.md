# Allocation-test assertion reachability

The current census has 34 nonpassing allocation-related test names, accounting for 366 original configurations. Reviewing their complete bodies and callbacks found **no additional post-loop text or callback-flag assertion beyond the six already covered by the current 72-case diagnostic**. The original matrix remains unchanged.

The six completed tails are `test_alloc_dtd_default_handling`, `test_alloc_public_entity_value`, `test_alloc_notation`, `test_alloc_public_notation`, `test_alloc_system_notation` and `test_alloc_nested_groups`. They check accumulated text and/or dummy-handler flags. Flags alone do not validate exact arguments, ordering, multiplicity or content-model structure.

Outside those 34 allocation-named bodies, `test_bypass_heuristic_when_close_to_bufsize` still fails its cumulative-allocation assertion before the final start-element count and remaining tuples. The other 11 configurations skip its body. The existing rejected pressure experiments should not be mistaken for a passing original memory-growth contract.

Potential new value tests should be labeled as additions. For example, `test_alloc_long_attr_default_with_char_ref` never checks the delivered 1,018-byte normalized default, and `test_alloc_long_attr_value` never checks its expanded attribute bytes. The child-encoding fixture similarly has no character-data assertion. None is a hidden unchanged assertion that simply needs a higher retry ceiling.

Permanent C source and prior six-case raw results are also archived. These are bounded coverage conclusions, not evidence of a newly observed parser defect or production readiness.
